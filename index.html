The Black Domain

The Black Domain

Overview & Culture

A demon nation built on a wound. The Black Domain occupies the western wasteland where the largest battlefield of the Primordial War poisoned the soil beyond recovery — black earth, no agriculture, no mineral wealth, and a sky locked in deep red or dark purple. The nation survives entirely on what it kills.

The public doctrine is absolute: the New World is a false prison, and the goddesses who stitched it are the reason the world is diminished. The private mandate is older — prepare the world for the return of the Goddess of Ending, as commanded by the Goddess of Abyss before her death. Only the First King knows the full weight of it, and perhaps Azalea, who wears the crown.

The mandate was invented
Secret

A silence that answered nothing

What almost nobody knows is that the mandate was invented. Ending never asked for the world. Abyss decided on her own that the world which had rejected her maker should be conquered and offered to her, and she died believing she was owed it. Every demon alive serves a purpose that may have been a grief-stricken goddess talking to a silence.

Life is clipped command and rare laughter. Children train before they can walk, drawing on genetic memory of thousands of years of combat instinct. Parents sharpen rather than teach. Death in battle is honor; death in bed is disgrace. Every part of every monster is used, and rationing is permanent and never questioned.

The nation is a coalition held together by mandate and fear: true demons, pure demons, ancient demon lines, the dark elves who chose survival, radical orcs who never stopped fighting, exiled dragons who followed Zar’goth, and the vampires who embrace what Noctisia suppresses. They share a purpose and very little else.

The Black Domain

History

Before Ruinea

The Goddess of Abyss was the only deity who could hear the sealed Goddess of Ending. She learned the truth of the sealing, and she decided on her own that the world which had rejected her maker should be conquered and offered to her. Ending gave no command.

Abyss shaped the demons as the instrument of that decision — living grudges created to win a war no other race was made for. The Dark Gods joined her banner, drawn by shared ambition, and the demons fought beneath it for forty thousand years while the First World came apart around them.

At the war’s end, the Goddess of Light and the Goddess of Abyss destroyed each other. Before dying, Abyss commanded the strongest of her creations — the First King — to continue the work and prepare the world for Ending’s return. The command was hers alone.

The demons survived the breaking on drifting fragments alongside ten other races, and they carried the mandate into the New World.

Ruinea

0–1500 EK — The Long Preparation

Fifteen centuries of arming

The First King ruled the Black Domain for fifteen centuries. Demons, vampires, radical orcs, and the Lost dragons lived under his authority while the nation armed itself for a war of conquest against a world still stitching its own wounds. Generation after generation trained for an invasion that had not yet begun.

500 EK — The Sundering

Vladimir’s refusal

Vladimir the Sunderer concluded that the First King was not leading his people to victory but to a glorious grave. He gathered the vampires who still held a choice and refused to follow the crusade any longer. The rebellion was quiet, sudden, and absolute. Vladimir led them south across the sea to a vast island in the Twilight Zone, and Noctisia was founded on the principle that thirst is not destiny. The Black Domain called it treason. Vladimir called it the first rational decision he had made in eight thousand years.

1500–1715 EK — The First Great War

Two centuries of advance

The First King launched the invasion with demons, radical orc contingents, the Lost dragons, and every faction that had chosen destruction. For two hundred years the horde advanced, and nearly half the continent fell under his banner. The Valley of Kings was breached, the Primeval Forest burned at its edges, and the surviving nations consolidated into a single coalition out of necessity rather than alliance.

1700 EK — The Angels Return

Seven against the horde

Seven Fallen Angels — the last children of the Goddess of Light — joined the coalition. They had spent two thousand years diminished and hunted, and they marched anyway. Their arrival turned a defensive war into a counteroffensive.

1715 EK — The Battle of Malakh-Kar

The King falls

The coalition struck at the demon capital. The Fallen Angels, the Dragon Lord and his war-flight, the Elder Elf Lysiane, and Vladimir of Noctisia held twelve demon commanders in the field while a party of heroes broke through into Malakh-Kar itself and brought the First King down. Three Fallen Angels died in the holding action — Lyurell, Cyanelle, and Avriel. A handful of demon commanders survived. The horde broke, and the invasion ended.

1715 EK — The New Crown

The Unraveler takes the throne

The Unraveler took the throne of the demon king. She gathered the surviving demons and led them home to the Black Domain rather than pressing a war her people could no longer win. What she inherited was a war machine with no war; what she built was a nation. She reorganized the territories, appointed four new commanders — Alleen, Silphira, Vexia, and Nuva — and converted the logistics of conquest into the logistics of survival. The satellite system, the layer structure, and the closed economic units that feed the Black Domain today are her design.

1730 EK – present — The Long Siege

A thousand years of stalemate

When the four surviving Fallen Angels marched west and founded the Theocracy directly on the Black Domain’s border, the war resumed in a form that has never ended. A thousand years of unbroken siege along the crescent: no advance, no retreat, no peace. Generations have known nothing else, and the doctrine of destruction has hardened into something the crown can no longer fully control.

Present — The Hidden Fault

The question nobody asks aloud

An underground movement founded by Vexia questions eternal war. Discovery means death. It has not been discovered. And beneath it all sits the older secret — that the mandate the First King still serves was invented by a grieving goddess who was never given an order at all.

The Black Domain

Geography

A 3,000 km × 6,900 km dome at the far western edge of the continent, curved like a closing bracket and separated from the settled lands by the Theocracy’s crescent and the Western Seam Zone. The interior layers in concentric depth: the eastern and northern rim touch the frontiers, the central belt holds the commanders’ cities, and the far western reaches hold the capital and the poisoned heart of the old battlefield.

The land is black soil over broken basalt, cut by dry ravines and fields of glassed rock. No rivers run. Water comes from deep wells, seasonal rain caught in cisterns, and melted ice hauled from the far north. Nothing grows in the soil, and nothing has grown there for tens of thousands of years.

The sky never clears. Deep red at day, dark purple at night, with dust that hangs in the air and turns the sun into a smudge. Visibility is short and light is scarce — conditions demon eyes and vampire senses handle comfortably and that no invading army has ever enjoyed.

The western, southern, and northwestern edges meet the sea. Coastal cliffs drop into deep water where leviathans range beyond the shelf, and the Shore-Cities live off what can be taken from it.

The Black Domain

Population & Hazards

Races

Demon-dominant, four generations

True, Pure, Ancient, and New Gen demons. Alongside them: dark elves, radical orcs in significant numbers, the Lost dragons who followed Zar’goth, and Black Domain vampires who wear their nature openly.

Monsters

Exceptional

Average Tier 3–5 with a heavy concentration of Tier 6–7 threats. The wasteland produces predators that would be apex anywhere else, and demons hunt them as daily survival.

Dungeons

Constant manifestation

Lower-tier manifestations appear constantly; Middle-tier dungeons open periodically. Demon war-parties descend them for cores, relics, and training.

Behemoths

High population, mostly Greater

Five Ancient behemoths and one Walking Mountain range the interior. Demons route around them, and behemoth migration patterns shape where cities are built.

The Black Domain

Government & Politics

Absolute military dictatorship layered by geography. The Unraveler (Tier 8.9) holds absolute authority over eight commanders (Tier 7–8), and the arrangement of their territories is itself a political instrument. A ninth seat exists by the Unraveler’s personal decree, and the active route determines who fills it.

The Three Layers

The Core

West — safest ground

Malakh-Kar and the poisoned heartland. The First King slumbers here, and the Unraveler rules from the fortress-capital above him. Farthest from any enemy.

The Second Layer

Central belt — interior functions

The seats of Alleen, Vexia, and Silphira. Strategy, espionage, psychological warfare. Their cities sit astride every road and supply line between the capital and the frontier. Whoever holds this belt decides which army eats, and nobody says it aloud.

The Frontier Layer

Eastern & northern rim — 100–300 km from the borders

The seats of Kazagar, Gorath, Vorlag, and Zar’goth. The radicals hold the war, the glory, and the dying, and every one of them can see the enemy from their own walls.

The arrangement is deliberate. The radicals receive honor and danger; the loyalists receive food and roads. Azalea built it this way, and every commander at the frontier understands exactly what that means.

Additional Seats

Nuva

No territory

She moves through the corridors between cities and answers only to Azalea. Nobody can map her movements, and nobody has tried twice.

The Commanders’ Council

Convened at Malakh-Kar

Summoned by the Unraveler. Rare, formal, and dangerous. Every meeting is watched by everyone.

The Revisionist Faction

Vexia’s hidden creation

It questions eternal war, knows nothing of the true mandate, and exists because stagnation bored her.

The Black Domain

Military & Defense

Every demon is a weapon. The nation keeps no separate standing army because the population itself is the army, organized into war-bands under commander authority and mobilized by decree.

Frontier War-Bands

Permanent garrisons

Along the Theocracy seam under Kazagar, Gorath, Vorlag, and Zar’goth. Rotating assault groups, siege beasts, and aerial raiders. The most experienced soldiers in Ruinea, forged by a thousand years of stalemate. They bleed at the border while the throne sits three thousand kilometers behind them.

War-Beasts

Aether-twisted predators

Bred in the satellite cities — siege-bred titans for breaking walls, fast packs for raiding, pack-hunters for the wastes. Demons breed weapons rather than tame animals.

Aerial Wing

Wyverns & lesser dragons

Under Zar’goth’s beastmasters, used for long-range supply, scouting, and terror raids. Reduces travel from months to days.

Berserker Orcs

Shock troops

Radical orc contingents who enter the berserker state with abandon, used as shock troops and breach-makers. They consider the war a form of worship.

Dark Elf Support

Logistics & cultivation

Logistics, cultivation, record-keeping, and the black-forest agriculture that feeds the frontier. Dark elves keep the front ranks supplied, and the front ranks stand on their ledgers.

Vampire Night Companies

Deployed after dark

Black Domain vampires in Feral Form, deployed after dark for shock assault and pursuit. Feared by their own allies.

Riderless Assets

Untamed terrain

The five Ancient behemoths and the Walking Mountain are terrain that occasionally moves. No commander has ever tried to command one.

The Black Domain

Technology & Transport

Technology & Magic

The Black Domain rejects external mechanical progress by doctrine. Aether use stays limited to basic survival — lamps, stoves, wells, and field tools. Dwarven forges, factories, and rails belong to other nations.

What replaces them is biological mastery. Selective breeding and surgical manipulation of monsters produce engines, haulers, and weapons that walk. Demon-forged weapons come from monster bone, obsidian, and salvaged dungeon steel. Every carcass becomes food, tool, armor, or bait.

Infrastructure & Transport

Paved Asphalt

Malakh-Kar only

The single paved surface in the nation, laid across the capital’s inner districts. Everywhere else is broken stone, dust track, and ravine.

Tamed Monster Haulers

Heavy logistics

Massive beasts pulling trailers of ore, meat, and siege material. The primary heavy logistics of the nation, moving at the pace of the creature and the terrain.

Demon Portage

Medium & light logistics

Medium and light logistics move on demon backs or on pack-beasts bred for endurance. A demon in monstrous form carries loads that would require a wagon elsewhere.

Riding Beasts

Civilian & messenger

Civilians and messengers travel on tamed predators suited to the wasteland — fast, vicious, and loyal through discipline alone.

Aerial Transport

Wyverns & dragons

Wyverns and lesser dragons carry supplies, messages, and passengers across distances that would take months overland. The only fast route between the layers.

Cistern Roads

Water routes

Marked routes between deep wells and water caches. Every journey is planned around them, and every commander guards their stretch.

Foot

The default

The default for everything else. Demons walk, and they walk far.

The Black Domain

Economy & the Satellite System

Food Self-Sufficiency

Full — through monster meat

Nothing grows, so everything is hunted, butchered, and rationed. Every settlement processes its own kills, and no region depends on another for survival.

The Satellite System

Closed economic units

Every commander’s city is supported by a ring of satellite settlements within 100–200 km, each specialized and self-sufficient. One hunts, one butchers and tans, one forges weapons, one breeds war-beasts, one renders alchemical reagents, one holds the cisterns. A city and its satellites form a closed economic unit that survives alone indefinitely. There is no single throat to cut.

Exports

Effectively none

Scavenged and stolen goods circulate internally. Universal agreement among every other nation bars trade with the Black Domain, and the Domain has never needed it.

Imports

None

Whatever arrives comes as plunder from raids or salvage from dungeons.

Internal Economy

Barter

Metal, cores, meat, and breeding stock serve as currency. Coin exists and is hoarded rather than spent.

War Economy

Frontier consumes most production

Weapon-forging and beast-breeding satellites run continuously, and every commander measures wealth in how long their war-band can fight without resupply.

The Black Domain

Key Locations

The Core

Malakh-Kar

Capital · pop. 90,000

The fortress-capital, raised over the chamber where the First King sleeps. Paved asphalt streets, the only paved ground in the nation. The Unraveler’s throne, the Commanders’ Council hall, the breeding pens of the true bloodlines, and the sealed vaults beneath. Every one of them knows what lies under the floor.

The Second Layer

Verathiel

Alleen’s seat · road nexus

A city of straight roads and perfect geometry where every route from the capital meets every route to the frontier. Warehouses, ration depots, and the quartermaster corps. Alleen holds it from a palace with no fortifications, because she has never needed them.

Silkhaven

Vexia’s seat · crossroads

A crossroads city of taverns, brokerages, and quiet rooms, where information is bought and identities are rented. Vexia’s shadow network runs from here and no one has found its edges. Lady Violetta keeps a townhouse on the eastern side.

Vaelthorn

Silphira’s seat · dead valley

An isolated spire-city reached by one road. Moth-wing banners hang from the walls and the corridors are arranged to make visitors lose their sense of direction. Silphira receives guests here, and most leave changed.

The Frontier Layer

Nightreach

Vorlag’s seat · northern seam

A black citadel where the Gar-Vang frontier meets the Theocracy curve. Vorlag holds the border, receives no orders after dark, and has never been visited by the Unraveler.

Emberhold

Kazagar’s seat · volcanic vent

A fortress built into a volcanic vent on the southern front, where the ground is warm and the air smells of sulfur. Kazagar holds the deepest advance into Theocracy territory.

Stonefall

Gorath’s seat · cracked basalt

A walled city beside Emberhold, held by the mountain-sized commander and his war-bands. Gorath and Kazagar fight as a single front and have never been separated.

The Aeries

Zar’goth’s seat · mountain roosts

A network of mountain roosts spread along the central seam. Wyverns and lesser dragons launch from here for raids, supply runs, and the patrols that keep the Theocracy’s sky empty. Zar’goth keeps no palace and sees no reason for one.

Outside the Structure

Shore-Cities

Coastal ports

A chain of fortified ports — Ashcliff, Tidesunder, Saltmarch — dedicated to hunting deep-sea monsters and processing marine carcasses for oil, hide, and meat. Poor, hard, and largely unsupervised. The best place in the nation to disappear.

Wanderer Camps

Exiled demons

Hidden encampments of exiled demons with broken horns, surviving in the ungoverned spaces between layers. They hunt, trade quietly, and move often. Discovery means death.

The Poisoned Heartland

Old battlefield

The far west, where the ground is glass and the residue is thickest. No permanent settlement exists within 500 km. Dungeon expeditions stage from here and return changed.

The Black Domain

Settlement Pattern

Every community exists to feed, arm, or house the war. Population clusters in the three layers; the spaces between them are empty and dangerous.

Tier 1 — Malakh-Kar

Capital & fortress

Population 90,000.

Tier 2 — Commander Cities (7)

Verathiel, Silkhaven, Vaelthorn, Nightreach, Emberhold, Stonefall, the Aeries

Population 20,000–50,000 each, with a full war-band command, a ration depot, and a breeding pen.

Tier 3 — Satellite Cities

3–8 per commander city

Specialized settlements of 3,000–15,000 within 100–200 km of their commander city, each dedicated to a single function — hunting, butchering and tanning, weapon forging, beast breeding, alchemy and reagents, or cistern keeping.

Tier 4 — Hunting Camps & Cistern Posts

5–12 per satellite city

Semi-permanent settlements of 100–1,000 on game migration routes or at deep wells. Rotating population, permanent guard.

Tier 5 — Wayposts & Watch Roosts

Routes & frontier

10–200 people on the routes between layers and along the frontier. Aerial roosts are staffed by Zar’goth’s beastmasters.

Naming conventions: demon settlements take hard, functional names — Emberhold, Stonefall, Nightreach, Vaelthorn, Ashcliff. Satellite settlements take a task: Render’s Rest, Cistern Nine, Breeder’s Fold, Bonemarket, the Tannery Flats.

Shape: large dome, curved like a closing bracket. Land access exists only from the east and northeast.

South / Southeast
